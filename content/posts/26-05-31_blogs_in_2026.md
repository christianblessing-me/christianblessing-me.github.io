Since the rise of social media the perceived number of actively maintained blogs declines. Human beings usually chose the path of least resistance, in the 2000s that was hosting a blog (most likely Wordpress) on bare metal, upcoming VPS service providers or classic webspace offerings where a (low-powered) hosted machine was able to run PHP and a MYSQL database. We did not call this the 'Cloud' yet but in practical terms this was the predecessor of what we are calling the cloud nowadays: a machine that's not yours in the internet running your code. Today's path of least resistance is publishing content on social media. The accessability and low entry barrier seem to be the main driver for handing over handcrafted (or slop) content to the big players in the market for human's attention.

## My 'content creation' story

Since I was a teenager (and online) I had the urge to participate and create content. We did not call this 'content' yet, it was more contributing to the internet community where I learnt the basic skills necessary. I wanted to give back. Since then I had numerous blogs (from which none is sadly still online) and was active in online communities (mainly boards, does anyone still remember [this](https://www.phpbb.com/)?). 

Build my first website with [Microsoft Frontpage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_FrontPage) where the base was a Photoshop file I designed. One could slice out the parts where the iFrames were supposed to be rendered later - CSS-layout were on the horizon back then but not crazy prominent yet. Hosting was done via a [GeoCities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GeoCities). No deployment pipeline. Files got transferred via [WS_FTP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WS_FTP) and I was crazy proud when I registered my first .org top-level domain so that I could ditch that weird [.de.vu domain](https://www.makerpendium.de/wiki/.de.vu-Domains) I had before.

Photoshop Tutorials were popular (no YouTube, nobody invented this yet + the bandwidth would have been way too low to stream videos) and shared in boards. I read a lot of them, learnt a lot, also created some and posted them on my blog. I was hooked on photography, got my first camera, got influenced by [deviantart.com](https://www.deviantart.com/) (no Instagram around) and started to post the pictures I took and edited on my own blog.

## Why am I telling this story about late 90s to mid 00s content publishing?

Pine for the past is useless but, we can learn from the past to improve the future. Looking at the trend of publishing to platforms where users ulimately don't have full control over their content as they cannot control the platforms does not seem like a wise idea. Platforms are subject to change. Even if it is right now unlikely but if Twitter, Facebook, Instagram or whatever shut down the content is lost, together with the reference where one could find it.

That also holds true for self-controlled platforms: remember that the content from my early days is also not available anymore, but that was my concious decision to take it down. Nobody would have stopped me from backing it up and hosting it still today. I could own my first top-level domain still today, the references would still be fine if I would have actively maintained them.

That is why I want to encourage the plan of hosting your own content. May it be a blog, may it be a podcast or anything else.
